# 02 · Best e-commerce experiences across industries

Phase 1, stream 2. Researched 27 Oct 2026. Ideas only: never copy assets, code or copy.

**Method note.** This session's network policy blocked direct page loads, so each site was confirmed live through fresh (Oct 2026) search-index results for its own pages, plus 2026 award listings. Eyeball the visual claims before Gate 1.

## 1. Summary: what separates premium from template

1. **One variable at a time.** Every variant shot at the same angle and scale, so colour or size is the only change, and only real combinations offered (Apple bands, Nomos).
2. **Specs are the design.** Exact numbers in the same order on every product (Arc'teryx, Field Notes, Peak Design). In 2026 the same structured data also feeds AI shopping agents through Shopify Catalog.
3. **Honest price architecture.** Per-unit price and real pack savings in plain view (Bombas, Who Gives A Crap, Sticker Mule). No MSRP strikethroughs, pop-ups or countdowns (Asket, McMaster-Carr).
4. **Guided choice beats more choice.** A short finder or headcount calculator (The Ordinary, Branch), plus one explainer page for the hero feature (Mejuri's vermeil page).
5. **Restraint in chrome and colour.** One owned colour used as a signal (Tiffany), glass only on the header and focal moments (Apple). 2026 award winners lean on effects-led spectacle: fine for one hero moment, never for the buy flow.

## 2. Findings by industry

**Award galleries (2026).** Awwwards e-commerce Sites of the Day (Outfit, Bucks Sauce, Serotoninn) and CSSDA winners (Heirest, Floema) skew to effects-led campaign sites (Heirest is WebGL); none is a bulk or B2B store. Borrow polish, not structure.

**Fashion.** Asket and Arc'teryx win on honesty and spec discipline (see table). Rains keeps a shopping bar sticky on mobile; Totême proves Shopify Plus can carry luxury. Wrong for us: model-led editorial, drop culture.

**Jewellery.** Mejuri explains its material with exact numbers (2.5 micron 18k gold over sterling silver). Tiffany applied one colour so consistently it became a trademark. Wrong for us: WebGL try-on (Heirest).

**Watches.** Nomos and Baltic (see table). Christopher Ward's strap pages offer width, colour and buckle choices: the closest analogue to choosing a lanyard. Wrong for us: heritage serifs, scroll theatrics.

**Beauty.** Aesop's sizes are plain text (500 mL, 500 mL refill); The Ordinary names products by what they are. Wrong for us: Aesop's warm amber palette (reject list), Rhode's founder-face imagery.

**Consumer tech.** Apple keeps glass on the nav and a sticky Buy bar; Peak Design rates hardware in numbers (90 kg per Anchor). Wrong for us: Teenage Engineering's quirk, Nothing's dot-matrix and red.

**Furniture and home.** Branch sells to office managers (headcount estimator, bundles, a "vs dealer" page). USM treats 14 colours as a system. Hem shows lead time on the product page.

**Premium DTC.** Bombas: 4, 8 and 12 packs at 10, 15 and 20% savings. Who Gives A Crap shows "$1.56 per roll" and runs wholesale tiers by cart total. Fellow has a compare page for close variants. Wrong for us: Who Gives A Crap's jokes.

**B2B and bulk.** Sticker Mule shows the saving per quantity (75% at 500); MOO has a Reorder section; Packlane updates unit price live; McMaster-Carr has no banners, pop-ups or carousels. Baymard's 2026 benchmark rates 75% of large mobile sites "mediocre", so a clean phone flow is itself a differentiator.

## 4. What lanyard and ID sellers do badly

Checked: ID Card Group, Specialist ID, AlphaCard, IDville, Uline, MyBinding, 4imprint.

- **Promo theatre.** ID Card Group's page titles read "Tournament Time Promo! $25 off orders over $250 with code MADNESS25". MSRP or "was" prices sit beside nearly every price (ID Card Group, AlphaCard, Specialist ID). It reads as a discount bin.
- **Part-number names.** "3/8'' Black 2137-2037 Adjustable Breakaway Lanyard w/ Hook"; all-caps titles at AlphaCard. Unscannable on a phone.
- **Unit confusion.** Some price per lanyard, some per pack; packs of 24, 25 and 100. AlphaCard shows one product at $129.99 on one page and $1.37 on another. Corpafly: always pack price plus per-lanyard price, same format.
- **Choice overload.** Dozens of near-identical widths, hooks and attachments, and nothing tells a teacher which to buy.
- **Vague safety.** Most just say "breakaway". AlphaCard is a rare exception, stating a release force (about 6 lb). Corpafly should publish its own tested figure once Muneeb confirms it.
- **Custom-print friction.** 4imprint lanyards start at 100 to 250 pieces, with proofs and setup on custom work. Corpafly's stock packs win on "in stock, no setup".
- **Worth matching:** IDville's plain cut-off line (same-day dispatch before 3 pm ET) and Uline's visible tiers, once MCF speeds are confirmed.
